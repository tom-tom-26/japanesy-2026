# Tokyo Itinerary — Thu 12 Nov to Sat 21 Nov 2026

Built for Mandeep, Tom & Jai. Base is **Shinjuku / Kabukicho** (per the accommodation guide), so every day is grouped to lean on the **JR Yamanote Line** and the Shinjuku hubs to cut transit time. Heavy-walking days are flagged; every restaurant/bar/attraction below is verified as a real, currently-operating, specialty venue (see the companion **Tokyo_Itinerary_Sources** file for citations on every claim). Follows the same **"NO SLOP" / zero-hallucination rule** as the Kyoto plan — nothing invented.

- **12→21 Nov (main stay, "the apartment" below):** 2-Chome-2-11 Kabukicho, Shinjuku City, Tokyo 160-0021, Japan.

**Dates (confirmed from the flight details in `japan_holiday_updated.html`):** the group returns from Kyoto on the Shinkansen **Thu 12 Nov** (arrival evening) and departs **staggered — Jai flies out Fri 20 Nov (Haneda 13:15), Mandeep & Tom fly out Sat 21 Nov (Narita 19:30)**. ### Day 1 (Thu 12 Nov): Arrival from Kyoto — Shinjuku home turf
*   **Late afternoon:** Arrive Tokyo Station on the Shinkansen from Kyoto (~2h15 on the Nozomi), transfer to Shinjuku (~15 min on the JR Chuo Rapid or Yamanote), drop bags at the apartment.
*   **Early evening:** Ease in with a walk through **Kabukicho** and the **Godzilla head** over Toho Cinema (right by your door) — no agenda, just get oriented on the streets you'll be walking home through all week.
*   **Dinner:** **Omoide Yokocho / 思い出横丁** ("Memory Lane," 5 min from Shinjuku West Exit) — a lantern-lit warren of tiny yakitori and motsu counters. *Specialty: charcoal yakitori & grilled offal at standing/counter stalls.* *Note: no reservations — walk the lane and duck into whichever counter has a couple of free stools; it's the low-effort, high-atmosphere arrival-night option.*
*   **Evening (Optional):** First taste of **Golden Gai / ゴールデン街** — ~200 micro-bars across six alleys. Start at **Albatross** (3 floors, chandelier-lit, one of the larger and most foreigner-friendly bars, ¥500 cover with a snack) so you're not fighting for stools on night one.
*   **Transit/Logistics Note:** Everything tonight is on foot from the apartment. Trains stop ~00:30–01:00; staying in Kabukicho means you walk home rather than pay for a taxi. Keep it light — you've got a travel day behind you and 8 days ahead.

**Backup / Rainy Day:** Both Omoide Yokocho and Golden Gai are covered-enough alleys and work fine in rain; if you'd rather stay dry, the restaurant floors of **Shinjuku Station** (e.g. inside Lumine/NEWoMan) are a dry alternative for a first dinner.

### Day 2 (Fri 13 Nov): Shibuya, Harajuku & Meiji Jingu (west Yamanote)
*   **Morning (08:30):** **Meiji Jingu / 明治神宮** (free, opens at sunrise ~06:20 in Nov, closes ~16:20) — Tokyo's grandest shrine, set in a forest of 100,000 donated trees. Go early for near-empty gravel approaches.
*   **Late morning (10:00):** **Takeshita-dori** (Harajuku's teen-fashion gauntlet) then the calmer, better **Cat Street / Ura-Harajuku** back lanes for streetwear and vintage.
*   **Lunch:** **Fuunji / 風雲児** (Yoyogi, ~8 min walk from Shinjuku's south side) — *Specialty: chicken-and-niboshi (dried sardine) tsukemen.* *Note: routinely named among Tokyo's best tsukemen; giant queue by noon and large-size noodles are free — arrive before 11:00 or after 14:00. Cash-friendly, tiny counter.*
*   **Afternoon (13:30):** **Shibuya Scramble Crossing** + the **Hachiko** statue, then **Shibuya Sky** (Shibuya Scramble Square rooftop, 229 m). *¥2,700 before 15:00 / ¥3,400 from 15:00; open 10:00–22:30, last entry 21:20.* **Book a ~15:30–16:00 slot** to catch daylight → sunset → city lights in one visit; slots are timed and sell out.
*   **Dinner & evening:** **Nonbei Yokocho / のんべい横丁** ("Drunkard's Alley," beside the Shibuya train tracks) — tiny two-storey bars and yakitori counters, a smaller and more local cousin of Golden Gai.
*   **Optional nightcap:** **The SG Club** (Jinnan, ~10–12 min walk from Shibuya Station) — Shingo Gokan's bar, nine consecutive years on Asia's 50 Best Bars. *Casual "Guzzle" upstairs is the walk-in-friendly floor; the "Sip" speakeasy below is the dressier one.*
*   **Transit/Logistics Note:** Shinjuku → Harajuku is 2 stops / ~4 min on the Yamanote; Harajuku → Shibuya is one more. You can walk the whole Meiji Jingu → Harajuku → Shibuya spine north-to-south, which is the efficient order.

**Backup / Rainy Day:** Shibuya Sky's open deck closes in bad weather (indoor floor stays open, partial refund logic applies) — swap in **Shibuya Parco** (Nintendo/Pokémon/Jump flagship stores) and the **Miyashita Park** complex, both covered.

### Day 3 (Sat 14 Nov): Tsukiji, Ginza & Tokyo Station (east/central)
*   **Early morning (08:00):** **Tsukiji Outer Market / 築地場外市場** — *Specialty: tamagoyaki, uni, grilled seafood skewers, standing sushi.* *Note: the inner wholesale market moved to Toyosu years ago, but the **outer** market (460+ stalls) is very much alive. Best window is 08:00–10:00 — half the top stalls sell out and shut by ~11:00. Closed Sundays and scattered Wednesdays, so **Saturday is a safe call.*** Graze breakfast across several stalls rather than one sit-down meal.
*   **Late morning (10:30):** Walk to **Ginza** (~15 min) — flagship-store territory. Worth a look even window-shopping: **Ginza Six**, the **Uniqlo** and **Muji flagships** (Muji has a huge one with a bakery), and the **Itoya** stationery megastore.
*   **Afternoon (13:00):** **Tokyo Station Marunouchi** — photograph the restored red-brick 1914 facade, then dive into the underground **Character Street / Ramen Street** in the station basement if you're peckish.
*   **Optional add-on:** The **Imperial Palace East Gardens** (free, closed Mon & Fri — open Sat) are a 10-min walk from Tokyo Station if you want green space.
*   **Dinner:** **Bar High Five** (Ginza, basement of the Efu Building) — *Specialty: bartender's-choice omakase cocktails, no printed menu, from legendary bartender Hidetsugu Ueno.* *Note: intimate, world-renowned; **reservations strongly advised** and there's a cover charge — dress smart-ish.* If you'd rather keep dinner casual first, do an early bite in Ginza and treat High Five as a post-dinner destination.
*   **Transit/Logistics Note:** Shinjuku → Tsukiji is ~20 min (Marunouchi Line to Ginza, then Hibiya Line one stop, or a single Toei Oedo ride to Tsukijishijo). Tsukiji → Ginza → Tokyo Station is all walkable in sequence.

**Backup / Rainy Day:** This day is largely rain-resilient — Tsukiji's lanes are partly covered, and Ginza + Tokyo Station are wall-to-wall covered retail and underground arcades.

### Day 4 (Sun 15 Nov): Asakusa, Ueno & Akihabara (shitamachi / old town)
*   **Morning (08:30):** **Senso-ji / 浅草寺** (free; grounds 24h, main hall 06:30–17:00) via the **Kaminarimon** giant lantern and **Nakamise-dori** shopping street — go before 10:00 to beat the crush. Optional **Tokyo Skytree** views are a short walk/train across the river.
*   **Lunch:** **Onigiri Asakusa Yadoroku / おにぎり浅草宿六** (behind Senso-ji) — *Specialty: hand-formed onigiri, Tokyo's oldest onigiri specialist (since 1954), a Michelin Bib Gourmand.* *Note: tiny counter, limited hours, can sell out — arrive early. Alternatively **Asakusa Imahan** for century-old sukiyaki if you want a bigger sit-down meal.*
*   **Afternoon (13:00):** Train to **Ueno** — choose your lane: **Ueno Park** museums (Tokyo National Museum is the big one) or the **Ameyoko / アメ横** market street under the train tracks for cheap eats, sneakers and general chaos.
*   **Late afternoon (15:30):** **Akihabara / 秋葉原** — Electric Town. Multi-floor arcades (**GiGO**, **Taito Station**), retro games at **Super Potato**, anime/figures on **Chuo-dori**. On Sundays Chuo-dori goes **car-free (pedestrian paradise, 13:00–17:00 Oct–Mar)** — good timing. The **Kanda Myojin** shrine (patron shrine of the district, tech-blessing amulets) is a 7-min uphill walk.
*   **Dinner & evening:** **Ebisu Yokocho / 恵比寿横丁** (Ebisu, ~2 min from station East Exit) — *Specialty: 20+ independent izakaya packed into one indoor alley — yakitori, oden, fresh fish, wine.* *Note: rowdy, elbow-to-elbow, brilliant for a group; ~¥2,000+/head, no bookings, just grab a table and hop stalls.* (Ebisu is 2 stops south of Shibuya on the Yamanote, easy loop back to Shinjuku after.)
*   **Transit/Logistics Note:** **Heavy-walking day** — Senso-ji, Ameyoko and Akihabara are three separate walkabouts. Asakusa → Ueno is ~5 min (Tokyo Metro Ginza Line); Ueno → Akihabara is 3 min (Yamanote). Save Ebisu for the very end since it's back on the west side toward home.

**Backup / Rainy Day:** Swap the outdoor market browsing for indoor time — the **Tokyo National Museum** (Ueno) and Akihabara's multi-floor arcade/retail buildings are all fully indoor; Ebisu Yokocho is a covered alley.

### Day 5 (Mon 16 Nov): Day trip — Hakone (onsen, Lake Ashi & Mt Fuji)
*   **Early (07:30–08:00):** **Odakyu Romancecar** direct from **Shinjuku → Hakone-Yumoto** (~85 min). Buy the **Hakone Freepass** (covers the whole loop: mountain train, cablecar, ropeway, pirate ship, buses) — grab it digitally via the EMot app or Klook to skip the morning ticket queue. **Sit on the right (seats C/D) for Mt Fuji views.**
*   **The loop (the classic clockwise circuit):**
    *   Hakone-Yumoto → **Gora** (Hakone Tozan mountain railway, switchbacks through the hills)
    *   Gora → **Sounzan** (cablecar) → **Owakudani / 大涌谷** (ropeway over a live volcanic valley — sulphur vents and the famous **black eggs** boiled in the hot springs; eating one is said to add 7 years of life)
    *   Owakudani → **Togendai** on **Lake Ashi / 芦ノ湖** → **pirate-ship cruise** across the lake to **Moto-Hakone**, with the **Hakone Shrine** red torii standing in the water
*   **Lunch:** on the loop — soba around Hakone-Yumoto/Gora, or the **Owakudani black eggs** as a snack en route.
*   **Late afternoon:** An **onsen** before heading back — **Hakone Yuryo** (near Hakone-Yumoto, has private-rental baths good for a group; note most public baths refuse large visible tattoos — Yuryo's private rooms sidestep that).
*   **Evening:** Romancecar back to Shinjuku (~85 min); easy dinner near home.
*   **Transit/Logistics Note:** **Reserve Romancecar seats in advance** (separate small fee on top of the Freepass) — it's a reserved-seat limited express. November is peak autumn-foliage season in Hakone, so the loop is scenic but busy; start early.

**Backup / Rainy Day:** Fuji hides behind cloud often — treat any Fuji sighting as a bonus, not the point. If the ropeway is closed (it shuts for high wind/volcanic gas, which does happen), the Freepass substitute buses run the same route, and the **Hakone Open-Air Museum** (indoor + covered galleries, Picasso pavilion) is an excellent all-weather anchor near Gora.

### Day 6 (Tue 17 Nov): Shimokitazawa & Nakameguro (trendy west/south)
*   **Late morning (10:30):** **Shimokitazawa / 下北沢** (7 min from Shinjuku on the Odakyu or Keio Inokashira line) — Tokyo's indie/vintage capital: no chain stores on the main lanes, more thrift shops than any district in the city. Hit **Chicago**, **Flamingo**, and **New York Joe Exchange** (a vintage store inside a former bathhouse; 50%-off sale first Sunday of each month).
*   **Lunch:** graze Shimokita's cafés and small eateries — it's curry, coffee and izakaya territory; nothing needs booking. Good third-wave coffee stops abound.
*   **Afternoon (14:30):** Train to **Nakameguro / 中目黒** — the cool-but-calmer canal neighbourhood. Stroll the **Meguro River** promenade (the cherry-tree canal; bare in November but the low-key café-lined walk is the draw), duck into design shops, galleries and the riverside **Starbucks Reserve Roastery** (its huge Tokyo flagship).
*   **Dinner & evening:** Nakameguro riverside **izakaya and yakitori stands**, then optionally back toward Shibuya (2 min on the Hibiya/Tokyu line) for a bar. If legs allow, this is a good night to return to **Golden Gai** properly now that you know the lay of the land — try **Bar Plastic Model** or **Cambiare** (both English-friendly).
*   **Transit/Logistics Note:** Deliberately a **lighter-walking, browse-paced day** to recover from Hakone. Shimokitazawa and Nakameguro are both a few minutes off the Shinjuku/Shibuya spine, so no long hauls.

**Backup / Rainy Day:** Shimokita's vintage shopping is largely indoor hopping between small stores; if the Nakameguro riverside walk is a washout, the **Starbucks Reserve Roastery** (4 floors, indoor) and Nakameguro's covered arcades hold up.

### Day 7 (Wed 18 Nov): West Shinjuku, Shinjuku Gyoen & a big cocktail night
*   **Morning (09:30):** **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building / 都庁** observation decks (Nishi-Shinjuku, **free**, 202 m) — a no-cost rival to the paid towers, plus the nightly **"Tokyo Night & Light"** projection show on the building's facade if you want to come back after dark.
*   **Late morning (11:00):** **Shinjuku Gyoen / 新宿御苑** (¥500; closed Mondays — open Wed) — one of Tokyo's best gardens, with Japanese, English and French sections; peak **autumn foliage** should be showing in mid-to-late November. A genuinely relaxing couple of hours 10 min from the apartment.
*   **Lunch:** back toward Shinjuku — this is prime **ramen** turf; if you skipped it, **Ichiran** (Kabukicho branch, solo-booth tonkotsu, open late/24h at some branches) or any of the West-Exit shops.
*   **Afternoon (Optional):** Depending on the group's taste — **Samurai/Ninja experience**, **golf/batting or arcade time**, or simply **Don Quijote Kabukicho** (the sprawling 24h discount megastore) for souvenir chaos.
*   **Evening — the cocktail crawl (Nishi-Shinjuku, all within walking distance of home):**
    *   **Bar Zoetrope** — *Specialty: 300+ Japanese whiskies, silent-film projections; opens 17:00, closed Sun.*
    *   **Bar Benfiddich** — *Specialty: Hiroyasu Kayama's foraged-botanical cocktails made tableside with a pestle and mortar; 9 seats, 18:00–03:00 Mon–Sat, closed Sun.* Regularly on Asia's/World's 50 Best — **get there at opening or expect a wait; no big groups without warning.**
    *   Optional splurge finale: **New York Bar**, Park Hyatt 52nd floor (the *Lost in Translation* bar) — skyline views, live jazz, **¥2,500-ish cover after ~20:00**. Smart dress enforced.
*   **Transit/Logistics Note:** Everything today is in Shinjuku — near-zero transit. Benfiddich and Zoetrope are both tiny (9–15 seats); with a group of three, arrive right at opening.

**Backup / Rainy Day:** Fully rain-proof if you swap Shinjuku Gyoen for indoor time — the Metro Gov observation decks, the ramen, the shopping and the bar crawl are all covered.

### Day 8 (Thu 19 Nov): teamLab Planets + Toyosu / Odaiba, last big night
*   **Morning (booked slot, ~10:00):** **teamLab Planets TOKYO / チームラボプラネッツ** (Toyosu) — *Specialty: barefoot, wade-through digital-art installations (water rooms, mirror-infinity spaces); open 08:30–22:00, last entry 60 min before close.* *Note: **timed tickets from ¥3,800, on sale ~late-August for November and they sell out — book the instant your dates are locked.** You get wet to the knee, so wear/bring shorts or roll-up trousers. Expanded in 2026 with new Athletic Forest / Future Park zones. Extended to run through end of 2027.*
*   **Lunch & afternoon:** **Toyosu Market** (the working fish market that replaced Tsukiji's inner market — public viewing decks over the tuna auction area + fresh sushi restaurants) is a short walk, or hop the Yurikamome to **Odaiba** for the waterfront: **teamLab-adjacent** bayside views, the **Unicorn Gundam statue** at DiverCity, and **Odaiba Seaside Park** looking back at the Rainbow Bridge.
*   **Dinner & final big night:** Back to **Kabukicho** for your last night on home turf. Options depending on energy: a proper **yakiniku/izakaya** group dinner, then a **Golden Gai** farewell round, or something loud — **karaoke** (Karaoke Kan / Big Echo, both in Shinjuku) or a **Kabukicho club**. This is the night to blow it out since Day 9 is departure.
*   **Transit/Logistics Note:** Shinjuku → Toyosu is ~30–35 min (Marunouchi Line to Ginza area, transfer to the Yurikamome or Yurakucho/Oedo routing). teamLab is a ~10-min walk from Shin-Toyosu station on the Yurikamome. Odaiba is the same Yurikamome line, so the whole day chains cleanly.

**Backup / Rainy Day:** teamLab Planets is fully indoor. If Odaiba's waterfront is a washout, its malls (**DiverCity**, **Aqua City**, **DECKS** with its retro arcade) are all covered.

### Day 9 (Fri 20 Nov): Jai flies home + a relaxed day for two
*   **Morning (Jai):** **Jai → Haneda for the 13:15 flight** (Haneda 13:15 → London Heathrow 19:10). Leave Shinjuku by **~10:00** — Toei/Keikyu via Shinagawa or the Airport Limousine bus from Shinjuku (~45–75 min depending on traffic). Say the goodbyes over an early coffee near home.
*   **Late morning (Mandeep & Tom) — pick one, both deliberately low-key:**
    *   **Option A — Old-town wander (no trains-heavy day):** **Yanaka / Yanesen** — one of the few districts to survive WWII bombing, so it keeps its shitamachi feel. Walk **Nippori Station → Yanaka Ginza** shopping street (street-food, "cat town") → **Nezu Shrine** (a mini corridor of red torii) → finish at Sendagi. Low-key, ~half a day.
    *   **Option B — Second day-trip:** **Kamakura** — the seaside temple town ~1 hr from Tokyo Station (JR Yokosuka Line, ~¥920). The **Great Buddha at Kotoku-in** (11.3 m bronze, standing since 1252; you can go inside for ¥50) + **Hase-dera** (Kannon hall, sea-view terrace, Benten cave), 5 min apart via the little Enoden line. Only pick this if the group still has day-trip energy after Hakone.
*   **Evening:** Last night as a pair — an easy Kabukicho izakaya + a final Golden Gai round.
*   **Transit/Logistics Note:** Keep Jai's checkout/bag logistics sorted the night before so the morning airport run is clean. Option A (Yanaka) is walking, not hauling across the city; save Option B (Kamakura) only if legs are fresh.
